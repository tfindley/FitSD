FitSD Fitness Review

A full review of the FitSD standard (v0.3, unreleased), followed by an end-to-end stress test: twelve role-played runs carried a fictional service — CertHub, at the invented Fernbrook Software — from idea through gates, build, acceptance, ten weeks of live support, a no-handover succession, and a governance retro.

1Verdict

FitSD does what it claims, where it claims it. The intake-to-acceptance spine — scope test, two gates, ratified SAC baseline, evidenced acceptance — worked in every seat that touched it, and several of its mechanisms demonstrably prevented real failures: the first test restore failed and was redesigned three weeks before go-live; the exit-path question asked at Gate 1 in 2026 turned a 2027 supplier EOL into a config swap instead of a crisis; the successor who inherited the service with zero handover could run it cold from the record set alone.

The structural weakness is equally clear, and every seat past go-live hit the same wall: the standard proves things once and never re-asks. Continuity is evidenced at acceptance and decays silently; conditions attached to decisions have no tracked afterlife (of eleven, three quietly died — one produced a 02:07 page, another an un-drilled successor); nothing obliges a live service to state service levels to its customers or report to anyone; and the annual review sat behind the entire failure curve, then itself silently failed. The fix was cheap — a handful of thin, event-triggered shalls, entirely in the standard's own idiom — and has since been approved and applied to the unreleased v0.3 (§9).

2What was done

  • Phase 1 — review, update, rewrite. Every document read; confirmed defects fixed (the Govern card's missing FSD-GV-7, five-vs-six capability drift, a broken Roadmap cross-reference, stale frontmatter, the untracked Inputs/Activities/Outputs page committed); house style settled and applied (TL;DR callouts, heading-numbering policy, complete frontmatter with documented semantics); prose pass (pitch deduped to one canonical home, dense paragraphs split). Commits 8bbe8ee, db7da1b, 2c88669.
  • Phase 2 — the simulation. Eight stages, twelve runs, each agent restricted to the standard plus the in-world records produced so far — never another participant's reasoning. The successor (Stage 7) was firewalled to the artifact set alone. Every participant logged findings in a fixed protocol, failures and worked-wells alike.
  • Phase 3 — synthesis. Findings consolidated and classified; all non-normative fixes applied (commit d5c977b); normative candidates drafted but not applied, per the evidence-first decision. Registers in simulation/findings/CONSOLIDATED.md and NORMATIVE-CANDIDATES.md.

6What demonstrably worked

The review should be as loud about these as about the gaps — several were praised independently from three or more seats:

  • The SAC baseline mechanism — "the most valuable hour of the workshop" at stand-up; answered the questions a lone engineer can't invent; made the acceptance decision "near-mechanical".
  • FSD-RR-6, the per-service incident profile — the desk's single best tool: two live severity calls made mechanically, including a Sev2 raised before impact for the founding incident's exact failure mode.
  • The evidence bars — "a test restore performed, dated" and "a test alert observed end-to-end" each caught a real defect before go-live; a proven procedure also turned out to be inheritable where an asserted one is not.
  • FSD-SC-4's exit-path question — asked at Gate 1 in 2026, it froze the consumer surface as a design commitment; the real 2027 supplier exit became a bounded backend swap. The standard's strongest single vindication.
  • The gate chain — four honest Gate 1 outcomes, a PoC box that bounds an experiment, a driver rule that protects risk cases from their value scores, and "don't design anything yet" doing exactly its job.
  • The record culture — honest, dated, evidenced records carried a no-handover succession "far better than any real-world inheritance", and gave the retro something to check.

9The normative package — APPLIED 2026-08-31

Six candidates were drafted with full wording and evidence (validation/scenarios/certhub/runs/2026-08/findings/NORMATIVE-CANDIDATES.md), all backward-compatible MINOR additions. The maintainer approved the full package; it landed on the unreleased v0.3 in commit 3862314 as FSD-GV-8, FSD-GV-9, FSD-SD-7 and FSD-SA-6, plus the note-level items (N4, N5, PS). N6 was implemented as an FSD-SA requirement — the lighter of the two options drafted. In evidence order:

N1 ⭐  FSD-GV-8 — owner transition / continuity re-verification

When the accountable owner of a live service changes or leaves, continuity shall be re-established and re-evidenced within a period the organisation defines: a handover, the successor's walk-through of the runbook and recovery procedure, any deputy or cover the baseline requires re-named, and the leaver's owned register entries transferred.

The successor's explicit "one change" verdict: this rule, fired in May, would have mechanically produced everything the first week lacked. A thin shall on a rare, unambiguous event.

N2  FSD-GV-9 — service levels & reporting

Each live service shall have agreed, recorded service levels — or an explicit best-effort statement — with its customers, published to them; and performance against them shall be reported to those customers at a defined cadence.

Promotes the standard's own BACKLOG candidates; closes FitSM PR2 + PR3 in one row. The framework currently requires withdrawing at retirement a commitment it never required making.

N3  FSD-SD-7 — conditions tracked to closure

Conditions attached to a gate or acceptance decision shall be recorded with an owner and a target date and tracked to closure; a breached condition shall be escalated to the approver who set it.

The forms now carry the fields (d5c977b); this is the duty that stops conditions silently dying. Body count: 5 closed on time, 3 late, 3 dead — one death paged a human at 02:07.

N4  GV-5 note — per-service review scope

Note-level on purpose: tightening the shall itself would be MAJOR under the Versioning Policy. Puts the review agenda where an auditor reads.

N5  SAC Documentation row — decisions survive delivery

"…design (carrying the key decisions and their rationale from delivery)…" — the successor called that log "the single most valuable page", and it only existed by an approver's invention.

N6  Cryptography / key management

Closes the self-identified NIS2 21(2)(h) gap. Lower priority; a bigger call, fine to defer.

PS  Two Requirements note-edits

FSD-RR-7 ("not retire — routed to the front door" is a legitimate record) and FSD-RR-6 (registration-evidence sentence). Non-normative, but they edit the normative doc — held for your OK.
