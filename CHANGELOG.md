# FitSD — Changelog

Notable changes to the **FitSD standard**, written for implementers. Newest first.

FitSD follows its [Versioning Policy](./FitSD%20%E2%80%94%20Versioning%20Policy.md): only a **normative** change (a new or altered "shall") moves the version. Entries tagged **[normative]** can affect conformance — if you already conform to the previous version, those are the lines to read closely. Everything else is new guidance, a clarification, or errata, with no version impact.

Changes are logged under **Unreleased** as they land; at release the heading is promoted to the version and dated (see [RELEASING.md](./RELEASING.md)).

## Unreleased

### Added
- **[normative] Every Service Acceptance Criterion is now *addressed*, by one of three routes** ([#20](https://github.com/tfindley/FitSD/issues/20)). FSD-SD-5 changes from "shall, as a minimum, **cover**" to "shall **address** every criterion — met against the standing baseline, met by an **approved alternative**, or **not applicable** under a baseline rule — recording the route and its rationale". The gap it closes: a criterion that genuinely applies but can't be met the standard way had nowhere honest to go. The backup platform isn't N/A for Backup — its catalogue and keys must still be recoverable — but it can't prove recoverability using itself; the same holds for the monitoring platform, the identity provider, the incident tool and the patching platform. These are **foundational services**, and the SAC reference now carries the standing rule they need: *a service that provides a SAC control to other services proves that criterion independently of itself.* An approved alternative is a Gate 2 decision with its reason recorded, not an open action — unless it's weaker than the baseline and time-bound, when it becomes a condition (FSD-SD-7). FRM-02 §5 and FRM-03 §1 gain a **Route** column; FSD-PRO §7 states the three routes; *Definitions* gains *approved alternative* and *foundational service*. The SAC reference also now names its **three layers** — fixed categories, the organisation's ratified baseline, and the per-solution *Service SAC* — which is where FitSM's own per-service acceptance criteria sit.
- **[normative] A new criterion: Validation & test.** Everything else in the Definition of Done proved a service could be *run*; nothing proved it *worked*. Gate 2 captured requirements as MoSCoW user stories and nothing ever looked at them again, so a service could pass acceptance in full while not doing the job it was funded to do. FSD-SD-5's enumeration gains *validation that the agreed requirements are met*. FRM-02 §2 gains a **How proven** column — which finally gives the glossary's long-orphaned *Given / When / Then* entry a home — and the Musts and accepted Shoulds are demonstrated at acceptance on FRM-03. The standing baseline holds the **method of proof, never the test cases**: how much evidence a service of this risk needs, who attests it, and whether whoever built it may attest their own work. New non-normative companion, **`reference/FitSD — Validation & Test Guidance`**: writing testable criteria, a proportionality ladder from a demonstrated walkthrough to an automated suite, who attests, environments, what counts as evidence, and re-validation at change (routed to FSD-CH-3 rather than a new mechanism).
- **A companion profile for teams already running FitSM** ([#19](https://github.com/tfindley/FitSD/issues/19), [#22](https://github.com/tfindley/FitSD/issues/22)). New **`reference/FitSD — Companion Profile — FitSM`**: a requirement-by-requirement mapping to FitSM-1 with a coverage column, the five things a FitSM shop still owes (FSD-SD-1…7, GV-7, GV-8, RR-6, RR-7), the form → FitSM record mapping, and the role mapping. **No second conformance route was added**, deliberately: *Charter* §7 already says you conform "with your own documents, tools and registers", and for such a team those documents are their management system — so a companion profile is an ordinary implementation profile whose satisfaction sources are another framework's requirement IDs. §7 now says so explicitly. FRM-01/02/03 gain one optional **Framework record(s)** header row so the two systems cross-link; the forms stay framework-neutral otherwise, because an engineer filling in a Gate 2 shouldn't have to read FitSM IDs.
- **The v0.4 preview line is open** (branch `v0.4`, served at `/0.4/` on the site). v0.3.0 stays current until v0.4 ships. The Roadmap says what v0.4 carries.

### Changed
- **FitSD's relationship to FitSM, corrected and reframed** ([#18](https://github.com/tfindley/FitSD/issues/18)). Two claims in *Standards Alignment* §2 were wrong against FitSM-1. The general requirements are **GR1–GR7** (17 requirements in 7 categories), not GR1–GR9 — there is no GR8 or GR9 — fixed in *Standards Alignment*, *Requirements* and the Govern card. And FitSM does **not** lack intake and design: PR1.2 requires proposals to be evaluated on predicted demand, resources and benefits; PR1.3 requires new services and major alterations to be planned, and says the plans "shall consider timescales, responsibilities, new or changed technology, communication and service acceptance criteria"; PR13.4 requires releases to be "built, tested and evaluated against acceptance criteria prior to being deployed". What FitSM leaves to the implementer is the *how*. The "only divergence" paragraph is rewritten accordingly — **FitSM requires the flow and leaves you to build it; FitSD builds it** — and the one genuine divergence gets its own line: FitSM's intake is scoped to *services*, FitSD's to all net-new work including the anti-sprawl technology trigger. The root cause is fixed too: §2 was mapped from secondary sources (blogs, an ITSM comparison, Wikipedia), so §7 now cites FitSM-1: Requirements, document version 3.0.1 (released 2024-05-21) as a primary source and names the release mapped against. The *Roadmap*'s "sharp wedge" note carried the same overstatement and is corrected.
- **FitSD says which of its two products you are adopting** ([#19](https://github.com/tfindley/FitSD/issues/19)). The content always behaved like a companion to an existing management system — every non-SD capability card says "FitSD ships no process; use FitSM PRx" — but the packaging read like a lighter *alternative*. *Charter* §1 now opens with what FitSD actually is: a Solution Development process, plus the thin spine it needs when nothing else is there. §2's FitSM row, §4's capability-model framing and *Adoption & Positioning* §6 and §7 follow; the *Implementation Guide* gains a companion path (your SMS already answers steps 1, 6 and 7 — step 4, the SAC baseline, is where the value is). Non-normative: no requirement changed.
- **SAC rows cite rather than compete, where another framework already owns the value** ([#21](https://github.com/tfindley/FitSD/issues/21)). Applying the reference's existing *Cite, don't copy* principle: the Availability row points at the SLA and the SAC *verifies* the target rather than setting it; the incident profile supplies service-specific triggers mapped onto the incident process's existing priority scheme and major-incident criteria rather than defining its own severities; Security and Access cite the standing policies; and a CMDB check is available to baselines that have a configuration practice to cite (it stays a declared non-goal standalone). FSD-GV-9's **note** is adjusted to match — "the availability SLO already lives in the SAC — or, where SLAs exist, the SAC verifies against them". Note text, not a shall.
- **The SAC baseline is a staged prerequisite.** Found by filling the new Idea Brief in for real. Its service-quality field assumed a ratified baseline, but the Quickstart (the path the Implementation Guide says to run first) never asks for one, so a team trying FitSD hit a field they couldn't honestly answer. The staging is stated once now, in *FitSD — Service Acceptance Criteria* ("How the baseline flows"). You don't need a baseline to raise an idea or to make the Gate 1 case: record *none ratified yet* and carry on. You do need one at Gate 2, and where none is ratified the Solution Owner drafts the rows that solution needs, with values people chose, marked *draft — unratified*. Acceptance is where it has to be ratified (FSD-GV-7); proven against draft rows, the record is a trial rather than a conformance claim. FRM-00 gains a *None ratified yet* answer, and FRM-02 §5, FRM-03 §1, FSD-PRO §6, the Quickstart, Definitions and the LLM skill follow. The skill used to stop outright when no baseline existed, which would have stalled an Idea Brief; it now carries on and says so. The Implementation Guide says to ratify before your first Gate 2 approval, and opens the front door at the end of that step rather than the one before. Non-normative: FSD-GV-7 is unchanged.
- **The Idea Brief (FSD-FRM-00) is restructured** ([#15](https://github.com/tfindley/FitSD/issues/15)). Filled in, it now reads in the order a reader needs: how things are, what's wrong with that, the questions it raises, and what "done" would look like. That's Barbara Minto's situation–complication–question–answer shape, from *The Pyramid Principle*, but the sections keep FitSD's own words rather than the four labels — a team running the intake for real found the labels got in the way of the thinking. v0.3's fields survive: *Problem / gap*, *Outcome sought*, the scope pair (now plainly **In scope** and **Out of scope**, with the hedge moved into the section lead), the service-quality expectation and the Next step block. Three things are new. A **Current state** line, because the as-is is worth stating flat before anyone argues about it. A **Risks** line, covering what goes wrong if it's left alone and anything obviously risky about doing it, with Gate 1 still doing the scoring and the recording. And a **Questions raised** table you add a row to, because a review asks its own questions and those answers were ending up in someone's notes instead of on the record. Answers stay outcomes: a product already in mind is a Gate 1 option, not an answer, and questions are kept open for the same reason. Gate 1's §1 carry-forward note maps the fields, and FSD-PRO, Definitions (new *SCQA* entry for the lineage), the Quickstart, Adoption & Positioning, Influences, the intake assistant and `llms.txt` follow. Non-normative: no requirement changed, and a brief written on the earlier layout is still a valid brief.
- **The validation suite moved to its own repo**, [`tfindley/fitsd-validation`](https://github.com/tfindley/fitsd-validation). It was `validation/` here. Non-normative throughout — no requirement, form or reference document changed, so this is errata and the version is unmoved. The reason is cadence: a run testing v0.3 can be contributed long after `v0.3.0` was tagged, so it could never be pinned to a spec tag, and every run added ~30 files of test evidence to the normative repo. The new repo also carries an AI **harness** (`harness/SKILL.md`), a run **validator** (`scripts/check-run.sh`) and a `CONTRIBUTING.md`, so third parties can run the suite against their own scenarios — by AI or by hand — and contribute the results. The v0.3.0 tag still contains the suite as it shipped.

## v0.3.0 — 2026-09-03

Theme: the Service Acceptance Criteria (SAC) become a **standing, organisation-owned baseline** — defined once and inherited by every solution, instead of rediscovered at each go-live. The supplier / supply-chain capability (FSD-SC) also lands, completing the capability model.

### Added
- **[normative] FSD-GV-7**: an organisation **shall define and ratify a standing Service Acceptance baseline** (its own thresholds for each SAC criterion) and apply it to every solution's Service Acceptance. This is the new conformance obligation for v0.3.0.
- **[normative] FSD-SC — Supplier & supply chain**, the conditional sixth capability: requirements **FSD-SC-1…4** (supplier register; assessment before adoption; expectations in agreements; periodic review with an exit path for critical dependencies) plus a capability card (`capabilities/supplier-supply-chain/`). Applies where services rely on suppliers or third-party dependencies the team is accountable for. Closes NIS2 Article 21(2)(d); maps ISO 27001 A.5.19–A.5.22 and FitSM PR8; adds the **supplier register** to *Information Stores*. A backward-compatible addition: existing conformance is unaffected.
- **[normative] FSD-GV-8 — owner transition / continuity re-verification.** When the accountable owner of a live service changes or leaves, continuity **shall** be re-established and re-evidenced within a defined period (handover, successor walk-through, deputy/cover re-named, owned register entries transferred). The headline finding of the 2026-08 end-to-end simulation: continuity was proven once at acceptance and decayed silently; the standard re-tested backups, never people. A backward-compatible addition.
- **[normative] FSD-GV-9 — service levels & reporting.** Each live service **shall** have agreed, recorded service levels — or an explicit best-effort statement — with its customers, **published to them**, with performance reported at a defined cadence. One thin `shall` closing the FitSM PR2 + PR3 gaps (*Standards Alignment* §2 updated); previously the framework's only normative SLA mention withdrew them at retirement.
- **[normative] FSD-SD-7 — conditions tracked to closure.** Conditions attached to a gate or acceptance decision **shall** carry an owner and a target date, be tracked to closure, and escalate to the approver who set them when breached. The v0.3 forms carry the matching fields. A new requirement, not a tightening of FSD-SD-6.
- **[normative] FSD-SA-6 — cryptography / key management.** Keys, secrets and certificates **shall** be managed to a defined standard (generation, storage, rotation, revocation) with evidence retained. Closes the self-identified NIS2 Article 21(2)(h) gap; maps to ISO 27001 A.8.24; the per-service posture lives in the SAC Security row.
- **`reference/FitSD — Service Acceptance Criteria`**, the SAC defined once, as inheritable, org-tunable principles, with a blank baseline template to ratify.
- **`FitSD — Implementation Guide`**, a business stand-up playbook: a 7-step checklist, the implementation-profile mapping (requirements → your local policies), and minimal small-team roles.
- **`FSD-FRM-00 — Idea Brief`**: the pre-Gate-1 intake is now a canonical form (numbered 00, ahead of the gates); it carries forward into Gate 1. Closes the "no Idea Brief form" gap (#6).
- **Gate 2 (`FSD-FRM-02`)**: a "Selected option + evaluation reference" field, the seam between the Gate 1 options and the Gate 2 design.
- **Tooling**: `scripts/check-sac-drift.sh` guards against a hardcoded SAC count creeping back into the docs; `scripts/check-version-drift.sh` keeps version claims out of governed docs' prose (the version lives in frontmatter, `VERSION`, this changelog, the README and the Roadmap, nowhere else).
- **`reference/FitSD — Inputs, Activities & Outputs`** — the whole framework on one page: per-capability inputs, key activities, outputs, and the records each touches. A synthesis, not new rules; the Requirements, Information Stores and FSD-PRO stay authoritative.
- **`llm/` — LLM integration layer** (non-normative). FitSD made usable *by* assistants and agents, interactively and semi-interactively: `llm/README.md` sets the ground rules (an LLM drafts, a human decides — no model is ever the Approver or an owner; evidence is real or absent; thresholds come from the ratified baseline, never the model; assistance is disclosed on the record) and the working patterns (draft-on-intake, the conditions sweep, acceptance linting in CI, review-pack assembly). Ships the portable **`fitsd` skill** (Agent Skills format — Claude Code ready, plain-markdown references usable by any model): stand-up facilitator, intake assistant, Gate 1 and Gate 2 reviewer/designer aides, acceptance auditor, conformance checker, and a service-review aide that operationalises the FSD-GV-5 walk, FSD-GV-8 transitions and FSD-SD-7 conditions. Root `llms.txt` gives models the curated reading map; `AGENTS.md` (+ a `CLAUDE.md` pointer) carries the rules for agents *editing* the repo — the streams, the guards, the style.
- **`validation/` — the fitness-test suite** (non-normative). A repeatable end-to-end stress test: a fictional organisation role-plays a project through every FitSD stage and seat, from submitter to firewalled successor, logging findings against the standard's actual text. Ships the protocol, thirteen role briefs, the findings format, the first scenario (**CertHub** at the invented Fernbrook Software) and that run's complete record — 16 in-world artifacts, 12 findings files, the consolidated register, the normative decision record and the report. This run is what drove the v0.3 simulation-hardening changes and the FSD-GV-8/GV-9/SD-7/SA-6 requirements above.

### Changed
- **SAC referenced by name, defined once.** `FSD-PRO §7`, `FSD-FRM-02 §5` and `FSD-FRM-03 §1` now reference the criteria from the new reference doc and carry only their stage column (design vs evidence). The criteria themselves are unchanged. A clarification, not a change to the Definition of Done.
- **`FSD-SD-5` note** now points at the SAC reference doc and names the standing baseline, including any recorded applicability rules.
- **SAC applicability: an honest N/A.** The standing baseline may define per-criterion *applicability rules*; the Service Acceptance Record (`FSD-FRM-03 §1`) gains an explicit **N/A per baseline (reason)** outcome. A recorded call against a standing rule, not a skip. A clarification of FSD-GV-7 / FSD-SD-5, not a new requirement.
- **One name for the flagship.** The capability formerly labelled "Bring in" is now **Solution Development** everywhere (Charter §4, README, Definitions, Requirements, both diagrams); "the front door" stays as its description.
- **The net-new effort line is yours.** The ~10-person-day intake trigger (`FSD-PRO §1`) is now the team's own **net-new effort line**, set once in the implementation profile; 10 person-days remains the suggested default. Set during stand-up (Implementation Guide, step 3).
- **Gate 1 lightened.** The value-lens **Total** row is gone (the lenses are a profile, not arithmetic); the form now says up front that §6–7 are conditional; `FSD-PRO §5` "what is captured" is a list instead of one long sentence.
- **Solo-operator guidance.** The Implementation Guide now says plainly what acceptance looks like on a team of one: there is no second pair of eyes, so the honest, dated, evidenced record is the control.
- **Version claims purged from prose.** Stale "v0.2" callouts removed from the Charter and Requirements; the README's status section now points at `VERSION` / this changelog. New style guard in `RELEASING.md`.
- **Requirements coverage note** aligned with the "sharp wedge" direction: the capability cards *are* the Tier 2 treatment for FSD-CH/RR/SA — FitSD builds no second full process.
- **Third-party & cross-team demand named (#10).** `FSD-PRO §1` now states the front door is **source-agnostic** — a third party, customer or another team can raise demand via the Idea Brief; the receiving team triages at Gate 1 and always holds the accountable Solution Owner. A clarification of the existing intake model (FSD-SD-1, Principle 3), not a new requirement.
- **The business case, named (#11).** Definitions gains a **Business case** entry and the Gate 1 form, `FSD-PRO §5` and *Adoption & Positioning* now name the **Gate 1 Outline Proposal as the business case**, the fundable proposal. No separate business-case document: the gate records already carry it (Principle 2).
- **Requirement notes clarified** (non-normative): FSD-GV-5 now says the review looks at per-service state (register accuracy, open conditions, overdue evidence, EOL candidates) and that a missed review escalates; FSD-RR-6 states registration is evidenced (accepted by the incident process's owner, by name and date); FSD-RR-7 admits "not retire — succession routed to Solution Development" as a legitimate recorded decision. The SAC **Documentation** criterion now carries the design decisions and rationale forward from delivery, and its baseline prompt asks how often documents are reviewed.
- **Simulation-driven hardening of the reference process and forms** (non-normative — an end-to-end simulation exercise, 2026-08, ran a fictional service through every stage and role; the findings drove these): each gate/acceptance sign-off now says **what the signature commits** (Gate 1 = design/PoC effort; Gate 2 = build + accepted run-cost, cash *and* operating effort; acceptance = the live service, its owner and its conditions); **acceptance may carry conditions**, each with an owner, target date and tracked home, and the record stays on the review agenda until they close; **acceptance names the live Service Owner** (and deputy) on the register — the Solution Owner hand-off recorded, not assumed; the forms gain **condition carry-forward/discharge rows** (FRM-02 §1, FRM-03 §2), a widened open-actions table with a *blocks acceptance?* column, a **service-register row** in the sign-off, an **ongoing operating effort** estimate line, RAIDD Owner/Status columns with an at-acceptance transfer note (risks → risk register, decisions + rationale → standing documentation), per-dependency §6 due diligence, a Status enum that matches the §8 outcomes, and pointers to *Definitions*; the Gate 1 Approver decides **with the demand register in view**; operator acceptance and incident-profile registration are **dated, named evidence**, first line included; the SAC reference gains support-model minimum prompts, a **decay note** (proven once is not proven forever), a cite-don't-copy rule and Owner-column semantics; *Information Stores* gains the **per-service operational documentation** store and homes for evaluation evidence and below-the-line enhancements; the Implementation Guide's stand-up steps prompt for the escalation line, alternate approver, T-shirt anchors, above-author ratification, an adoption record, and a review agenda that can't be skipped; Definitions' **SOP** entry no longer contradicts the Documentation criterion, and **Operator** is now defined.

Candidates only — no `shall` has changed.

Meaning unchanged throughout.
- **House style settled and applied.** Every standard document now opens with a `> **TL;DR** —` callout (the capability cards and the four forms keep their deliberate shared openers); FSD-PRO's `§n`-prefixed headings move to the corpus-standard `n.` form; frontmatter is completed corpus-wide (`type`, `version`, `status` on every tiered document, with the semantics of each recorded in the *Versioning Policy*, "Document markers"); the callout and section-citation conventions join the style guards in `RELEASING.md`.
- **Govern card completed for v0.3.** The `FSD-GV` card now carries **FSD-GV-7** (it had stopped at GV-6) and names the SAC baseline as Govern's **service design baseline** — a standing standard every solution is designed *to* at Gate 2 and proven against at acceptance (the framing settled in the Roadmap, "Shape & scope"). The *SAC baseline* entry in Definitions gains the same framing.
- **FitSM gap note added** (*Standards Alignment* §2): PR2 Service Level Management and PR3 Service Reporting named as candidate thin requirements, PR7 and PR11 as declared non-goals — the note the Roadmap's "Honest gaps" bullet already cited.

Stale `version: 0.2` frontmatter bumped on the docs v0.3 touched (Information Stores, Standards Alignment, Diagrams, the GV/CH/RR/SA cards). The README's "What's in here" table gains the missing rows (Versioning Policy; `VERSION` / `RELEASING` / `SECURITY`).

The **Supportability / handover** criterion now says **knowledge transfer / cross-training** in words and proves that any **new or changed standing procedure the service forced** (flagged at Gate 2 §4) is written and adopted before go-live, closing the design→prove gap. Non-normative: the nine SAC categories are unchanged; this works within Documentation + Supportability. Standing *policies* stay out of the per-service record (referenced out, not authored at acceptance).

### For implementers moving from v0.2
- The SAC **categories are unchanged** — nothing you accepted under v0.2 becomes non-conformant.
- **Ratify your SAC baseline** (FSD-GV-7). You were already choosing these thresholds at acceptance; now you set them once, up front, and inherit them into every solution.
- **Stand up the supplier register** (FSD-SC, if it applies to you, and it does if your services stand on cloud, SaaS or support contracts). The pre-adoption assessment was already in Gate 1 §6; the register and a periodic review folded into a cadence you already run are the new muscle.
- **Define your owner-transition period** (FSD-GV-8) — what happens, and by when, when a Service Owner moves on. One paragraph in your implementation profile; it fires rarely and matters most when it does.
- **Publish your service levels** (FSD-GV-9). If a service is genuinely best-effort, say so in writing to the people who depend on it — the statement is the requirement, not a number.
- **Track your conditions** (FSD-SD-7). If your gate decisions already carry conditions, give each an owner and a date and somewhere they're chased — the new form fields show the shape.
- **Point at your key-management practice** (FSD-SA-6) in the implementation profile; if you don't have one written down, the requirement is the prompt to fix that.
