# FSD-FRM-00 — Idea Brief

> **The Idea Brief asks: is this worth a proper look?** One page, in four moves: the **situation**, the **complication** that forces a decision, the **question** that raises, and your **answer**. That's SCQA, Barbara Minto's shape for a brief that lands, so the same page works read aloud as a two-minute pitch to a review forum. **Don't design anything yet**: your answer is the outcome you're after, not the build. Options and design come at the gates (Gate 1 makes the case, Gate 2 designs it). Once it's worth pursuing, this carries forward into Gate 1 (FSD-FRM-01) — and if you can already answer Gate 1's questions, go straight there; the brief is optional. Related demands may share one brief (the Gate 1 Approver decides whether they share a case). *(Form 00 — the optional pre-gate intake, before the numbered gates. Terms: see `FitSD — Definitions`.)* The live copy is held in the team's work-tracking system; this is the blank template.

## 1. Situation

Where things stand today: the ground your reader already agrees with. No problem yet.

| Field             | Entry                                                   |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |

## 2. Complication

What's changed, broken or now demanded, so the situation can't stand. This is the problem: state it, don't solve it.

| Field                              | Entry                                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Complication**                   | *The gap, pain, incident, obligation or opportunity forcing a decision* |
| **Demand source / primary driver** | *Where it came from and why: strategy, customer, compliance, risk, operational, finance/people; value / compliance / risk reduction* |

## 3. Question

The one question the complication raises: the decision this brief asks for. Keep it open, so more than one answer could fit. *"How do we stop certificates expiring unseen?"* leaves room for options; *"Should we buy tool X?"* has already designed the answer.

| Field            | Entry                                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Question**     | *The decision needed, in one sentence*                  |

## 4. Answer

Your answer to the question, as an **outcome**, not a design: what "done" looks like, and roughly where its edges are. Got a solution in mind already? Keep it for Gate 1, where it becomes one of the options (FSD-FRM-01 §5).
